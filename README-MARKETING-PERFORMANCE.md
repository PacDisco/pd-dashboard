# pd-dashboard additions — Marketing Performance

Adds a `Marketing Performance` dashboard that joins hand-entered ad spend to
the live HubSpot funnel, and shows cookie attribution alongside what applicants
actually say on the application form.

## Why this and not more of `lead-data-sheet`

`/lead-data-sheet/` already charts spend, traffic, leads, opps and clients per
channel per month — but every one of those numbers is typed into a Google Sheet
and read back through a hardcoded Apps Script URL, with no connection to
HubSpot. `/sales-funnel/` has the opposite problem: it reads HubSpot live and
knows nothing about cost.

Nothing joined the two, so nobody could answer *what did a sale cost on this
channel*. That join is what this dashboard is for. It deliberately reuses
`sales-funnel-data.mjs` rather than re-querying HubSpot, so the funnel
definitions can never drift between the two pages.

## Apply

1. Run `MIGRATION-marketing-spend.sql` against Neon (idempotent — safe to re-run)
2. Drop the files above into the repo
3. `npm run build` to regenerate the manifests, then commit what it produces
4. `npm run test:marketing` to confirm the page still renders (needs `playwright`)
5. Commit + push

**Heads up on step 3:** the committed `dashboards.json` was generated on
2026-06-16 and is stale — seven folders that already exist on disk
(`flight-programs`, `instructors`, `program-schedule`, `time-tracking`,
`ue-applications`, `ue-flights`, `unearthed-stripe`) are missing from it. The
rebuild picks them all up, so expect a larger diff than just this dashboard.
Those seven land as `General` / admin-only until they get their own
`dashboard.json`. Worth a look, but it isn't caused by this change.

## Comparing time periods

Tick **Compare to** and the whole page switches to two periods: every KPI gains
a percentage delta and a "was" figure, the cost table gains prior-period sales
and cost-per-sale columns with a change badge, and the trend chart overlays the
prior period as dashed lines.

Two presets derive range B from range A so the two windows are always the same
length — **Same period last year** (A shifted back twelve months) and
**Previous period** (the same number of months immediately before A). Both dates
stay editable if you want an arbitrary window. Comparing a 12-month range to a
3-month one produces deltas that look real and aren't, which is why B is derived
rather than typed by default.

Three details worth knowing:

- **Cost deltas invert.** A fall in cost per sale renders green, a rise red.
  Volume metrics use the opposite convention, because for them up is good.
  Colouring them the same way would be actively misleading.
- **A zero base reads as "new", not as a percentage.** Going 0 → 5 has no
  meaningful percentage; anything within ±1% reads as flat so noise isn't
  dressed up as a trend.
- **The prior period aligns by position, not calendar month.** That's the only
  way twelve months can sit against the same twelve a year earlier on one axis.
  The dashed styling and the axis labels (which stay on period A) keep it
  explicit.

A failure fetching period B is not fatal — you lose the deltas, not the
dashboard.

## Inside Offline Sources

Offline was the biggest bar on the cookie panel and said nothing. It isn't a
channel — it's what HubSpot writes when the tracking cookie never reached it,
which for Pacific Discovery means almost everything arriving through Zapier.

The real channel is usually still there, in
`original_source_drill_down_3/4/5`, where the form's `utm_source`,
`utm_medium` and `utm_campaign` land. The panel reads them back and maps them
to a channel. Over the twelve months to Aug 2026 that recovers **459 of 1,093
Offline leads (42%)** — the remaining 634 carried no UTM at all and are
reported as *Not recoverable* rather than guessed at. The greyed bar and the
italic table row exist so the split can never be mistaken for full coverage.

Two things about the mapping worth knowing before you trust a row:

- **`drill_down_3` is not reliably `utm_source`.** Sometimes it is
  (`adwords`, `chatgpt.com`); sometimes it's a HubSpot-side category with the
  vendor in `drill_down_4` (`Recruitment` › `TeenLife`, `referrals` ›
  `gooverseas.com`). Every rule in `OFFLINE_RULES` reads dd3 and dd4 together
  for that reason.
- **An unmatched value is shown, not swept up.** It renders as
  `Unmapped: <raw value>` instead of being folded into Other Campaigns. An
  invented channel is worse than a visibly unmapped one, and this is how you
  notice a rule is missing. Add it to `OFFLINE_RULES` when one appears.

The recovery rate rises from here: `gclid`, `fbclid` and the UTM fields were
only added to the application form and the quiz recently, so months before
that have thinner drill-downs than months after. This panel reads the damage;
fixing attribution at the point of capture is still the actual repair.

## Why "What applicants say" was almost entirely (Unknown)

Because it was charting the wrong denominator. The panel plots every lead, but
"How Did You Find Pacific Discovery?" is only asked on **step 2** of the
application (Jotform `240277257210046`, ~399 submissions). The ~2,045 PD leads
in a twelve-month window mostly arrive through the Gap Year Semester Quiz or
step 1 of the application, neither of which asks the question. Roughly four
out of five people in that chart were never asked — and they were all being
piled into one `(Unknown)` bar alongside the handful of applicants who genuinely
skipped it. One bucket, two opposite meanings, and it dwarfed every real answer.

So `(Unknown)` is now two labels:

- `(No application on file)` — no matching Jotform submission. **Excluded from
  the chart**, and counted in the line underneath it instead.
- `(Application left blank)` — they applied and skipped the question. Stays in
  the chart, because that's a real non-answer.

The line under the chart states how many answers came from how many applicants,
and how many leads were never asked, so the panel reads as *the ranking among
applicants* — which is all it was ever able to say.

## This changes `/sales-funnel/` too

`sales-funnel-data.mjs` is shared. Two behaviour changes land there as well:

- The Jotform source breakdown gains the two labels above in place of
  `(Unknown)`. That page renders labels generically, so nothing breaks — the
  one giant bar just becomes two honest ones.
- Each contact read now also fetches three drill-down properties. Same number
  of API calls, slightly wider payload.

The funnel totals, stage definitions and exclusions are untouched.

## Two decisions worth knowing about

**Reads are open, writes are gated.** `/api/*` sits outside the edge auth gate,
so `marketing-spend` verifies the caller itself on write via
`_shared/identity.mjs` and requires `admin`, `outreach` or `operations`. Reads
are left open, matching `sales-funnel-data.mjs`. The aggregate isn't sensitive;
the cost base is.

**The two attribution panels are not paired.** HubSpot emits channel labels
(`Paid Social`, `Referrals`); Jotform emits answer strings (`Social Media`,
`Go Abroad`, `Gap Year Association`). Putting them on a shared axis would imply
a row-to-row correspondence that doesn't exist. They render as two independent
rankings, and the page says so.

## Known limits

- **Ad spend is typed, not fetched.** Neither Google Ads nor Meta is connected
  to this stack, so you enter the monthly figure in the Ad spend grid at the
  bottom of the page — a cell per month per channel, then Save. Set each
  column's billing currency once from the dropdown in its header, and the
  USD → NZD rate in the box above the grid. That rate is seeded from whatever
  was last saved, never from a hardcoded guess; until it is set, USD figures
  are not converted and the Save button stays disabled rather than writing a
  zeroed NZD amount that would quietly erase a month's cost.
- **Blended cost-per only counts months that have spend**, and says so on the
  tile. Dividing two months of spend by a year of sales produces a number that
  looks precise and means nothing.
- **Original Source is currently unreliable.** Records arriving via Zapier land
  as `Offline Sources` with the real channel stripped. *Inside Offline Sources*
  recovers 42% of it from the drill-downs and states the rest as unrecovered.
  The dashboard reports the problem rather than hiding it.
- **The two attribution panels cover different populations.** The cookie panel
  covers every lead; the self-reported panel covers only leads with an
  application on file, because that is the only place the question is asked.
  Their totals will never agree and are not meant to.
- **A missing spend table degrades, it doesn't blank the page.** If
  `MIGRATION-marketing-spend.sql` hasn't been run, the funnel still renders and
  a notice names the migration to run. Spend is an optional layer, not a
  dependency.
- **Sessions can be unavailable.** If the HubSpot private app lacks the
  analytics scope the page shows a notice and carries on; every other figure is
  unaffected.
- **Charts degrade.** If the Chart.js CDN fails the tables still render and the
  page says the graphs are missing, rather than dying on a `ReferenceError`.
